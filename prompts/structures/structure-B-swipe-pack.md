# Structure B — Template / Swipe Pack

Extracted from "10+ Copy/Paste DM Templates (15%+ reply rate)" and its child pages.

**Use when:** the reader wants something they can use immediately without thinking.
The value is in the copy-paste, not the education.

**Title formula:** `[N]+ [Thing] Templates ([Quantified Proof])`
e.g. "10+ Copy/Paste DM Templates (15%+ reply rate)",
"Irresistible Banner (9 templates)", "Building a Magnetic Featured Section (4 Examples)"

The proof in brackets is not optional. It is what makes the title work.

---

## Page 1 — Cover

- Title with the number and the proof metric
- Who it's for, in one line
- Client name and handle

## Page 2 — How to use this

- Two or three lines maximum
- A **legend** if the pack uses symbols
  _Reference: ✍️ = Text Message · 🗣️ = Voice Message · 🤖 = AI Voice Message_

## Pages 3–N — One page per template

Each template page has a fixed anatomy:

**1. Context block (top of page)**

```
Who this is for:      [role / business type]
Target audience:      [who receives it]
Source these from:    [named tools or places]
Format:               [applicable variants]
```

**2. Approach name** — every template is _named_, not numbered.
"Direct Case Study Approach", "Pain Point Approach", "Social Sell Approach".
A named approach is memorable and reusable; "Template 4" is not.

**3. The template itself**, in a visually distinct block

- Variables in `{{double braces}}`: `{{first name}}`, `{{your name}}`, `{{link}}`
- **Bold every part the reader must swap** for their own situation
- Multi-step sequences are numbered in order (MSG 1 → MSG 2 → MSG 3)
  with the intent of each step implied by its content

_Reference pattern:_

> Hey {{first name}} — I like to be direct and don't want to waste your time:
>
> I work with **SaaS founders** on **LI** to help them **get their first 500
> monthly users** without **fundraising**. We helped **Luna** grow from
> **13 to 550 monthly users** in **6 weeks** and saw your platform is similar.
>
> Against me telling you how we did it?
>
> {{your name}}

Note what is bold: the audience, the channel, the outcome, the objection removed,
the client name, the numbers, the timeframe. Everything a reader must replace.

**4. Optional supporting note** — a screenshot, a tool tip, or a variant.

## Final page — CTA

- What to do next
- The client's offer, stated plainly once

---

## Rules for the generator

- **Every template must be usable with zero editing beyond the variables.**
  If the reader has to think, the template failed.
- **Name every approach.** Never "Template 1".
- **Two markup conventions, used consistently:**
  `{{variable}}` = swap this. **Bold** = this is specific to the example, change it.
- **Specific numbers in every example.** "13 to 550 in 6 weeks" beats "significant growth".
  Numbers come from the client's Knowledge Vault — never invent them.
- **Word budget: one template = one page.** If a template needs two pages, it is
  two templates.
- 8–12 templates is the sweet spot. Below 6 feels thin; above 15 feels padded.
